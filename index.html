What is a simple skincare routine?

Keep it basic: gently cleanse, moisturize, and use broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher during the day. At night, cleanse and moisturize. Add products slowly only if you have a reason to use them.

American Academy of Dermatology: basic skin care

In what order should I apply products?

A simple order is cleanser, any treatment product you already use as directed, then moisturizer. In the morning, finish with sunscreen. If you have a prescribed treatment, follow your clinician’s instructions.

AAD: applying skin-care products in order

How often should I wash my face?

A gentle wash in the morning and before bed is a common starting point; wash after heavy sweating too. Use lukewarm water and fingertips, and avoid scrubbing, which can irritate skin.

AAD: face washing 101

What are gentle basics for acne-prone skin?

Use gentle, non-abrasive skin care; avoid scrubbing, picking, and frequently switching products. Look for labels such as “non-comedogenic” or “won’t clog pores” when choosing sunscreen or moisturizer. If breakouts persist or cause pain or scarring, consider seeing a dermatologist.

AAD: tips for managing acne

What sunscreen should I look for?

For outdoor use, dermatologists commonly recommend broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher on skin not covered by clothing. If you are acne-prone, look for “non-comedogenic” or “won’t clog pores.” Follow the label directions.

AAD: sun protection and acne-prone skin

Can this camera tell me my skin type?

No. This camera feature only previews a photo. Lighting, filters, makeup, and camera settings can change how skin looks, and a picture cannot reliably tell how your skin feels. Notice how your skin feels during the day, or ask a dermatologist for personalized guidance.

Do I need moisturizer if my skin feels oily?

Oily-feeling skin may still benefit from moisturizer. Choose a product that feels comfortable and does not irritate you; if you are acne-prone, “non-comedogenic” or “won’t clog pores” can be useful label terms. SkinSight does not endorse brands.

AAD: moisturizer and acne-prone skin

What if a product irritates my skin?

Stop using a product that causes a concerning reaction and avoid adding more new products while your skin settles. If irritation is severe, persistent, or worrying, contact a healthcare professional. Follow product labels and any clinician’s advice.

When should I ask a dermatologist?

Seek professional advice for symptoms that are persistent, painful, worsening, or concerning, or if a skin issue affects your wellbeing. A photo FAQ page cannot examine your skin or replace an appointment.

American Academy of Dermatology patient resources
